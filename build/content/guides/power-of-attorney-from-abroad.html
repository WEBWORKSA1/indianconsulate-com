General vs special PoA

A general PoA gives broad authority; a special PoA covers one specific transaction. Many registrars and banks are cautious with general PoAs for property sales, so a special PoA naming the property and the act is often the safer choice.

The four stages

  1. Draft it to Indian requirements — ideally with a lawyer in the state where the property is, who knows local registrar practice.
  2. Sign it in front of the authenticating officer. Don’t sign in advance.
  3. Authenticate it, either at the Indian mission (consular attestation) or, if you live in an Apostille Convention country, through a local notary plus apostille — check which your registrar accepts.
  4. Adjudicate and stamp it in India: the attorney presents it to the stamp authority in the relevant state within the time limit (commonly three months of receipt in India) and pays stamp duty. Property-related PoAs may also need registration.

What to bring to the mission

  • The unsigned PoA (and copies), printed as the mission requires
  • Your passport and local status document
  • Photos where the format includes them
  • ID copies of the attorney and witnesses, if required

Mistakes to avoid

  • Signing before your appointment
  • Vague property descriptions — use survey numbers and full addresses
  • Missing the stamping deadline in India
  • Using a PoA format that the specific registrar or bank won’t accept
