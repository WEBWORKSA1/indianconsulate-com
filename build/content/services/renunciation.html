Key facts at a glance

SurrenderFor people who have already acquired foreign citizenship. Indian citizenship ended automatically; you surrender the passport and get a certificate
RenunciationA formal declaration by an adult Indian citizen who holds, or is about to take, another nationality
Why it mattersNeeded for an OCI, and often to close out Indian records, property and banking paperwork
Penalty riskUsing or keeping an Indian passport after taking foreign citizenship can attract penalties

Surrender vs renunciation — which one is yours?

Under Section 9 of the Citizenship Act, 1955, an Indian citizen who voluntarily acquires another country’s citizenship ceases to be an Indian citizen. Nothing has to be filed for that to happen — but the Indian passport must be surrendered, and the mission issues a surrender certificate as proof.

Renunciation (Section 8) is a voluntary declaration giving up Indian citizenship. It is typically used where another country requires proof that you have renounced before, or as part of, naturalisation. If you have already naturalised, you are usually in the surrender category.

Documents you will typically need

  • Your last Indian passport, in original — it will be cancelled
  • Your current foreign passport
  • Naturalisation or citizenship certificate showing the date you acquired foreign citizenship
  • Proof of address in your country of residence
  • Photographs to the mission’s specification
  • The completed online application and fee, plus any penalty that applies

How to apply

  1. Fill in the online application. Surrender certificates are applied for through the Indian missions’ online system (via the Passport Seva portal for missions in most countries); renunciation declarations have their own online form. Your mission’s website names the right one.
  2. Submit to your mission or its application centre with your original Indian passport.
  3. Pay the fee and any penalty. Penalties typically apply if the Indian passport was used for travel after you acquired foreign citizenship, and can increase with the length of time involved.
  4. Receive the certificate and your cancelled passport. Keep both safely — you will need them for your OCI and future paperwork.

What if my old Indian passport is lost?

Missions have procedures for lost or destroyed passports — usually a police report or affidavit plus the passport details you still have. Some missions accept alternatives in specific cases; for example, there are special arrangements for some people from the former Portuguese territories of Goa, Daman and Diu. Ask your mission before you apply.

How this connects to your OCI

If you ever held an Indian passport, your OCI application will normally ask for your surrender or renunciation certificate. Getting it first avoids a rejected OCI file and a second fee.

Frequently asked questions

I naturalised years ago and never surrendered my Indian passport. What now?

Apply for a surrender certificate now. You may have to pay a penalty, especially if you travelled on the Indian passport after naturalising — but leaving it unresolved blocks your OCI and can cause problems later.

Does surrender affect property or bank accounts in India?

You keep property you already own, but your residential status changes. Banking, investment and tax rules for foreign citizens of Indian origin apply. Speak to your bank and a tax adviser.

Can I get Indian citizenship back later?

There is a route to resume citizenship in limited circumstances under the Citizenship Act, but it is not automatic. For most people the OCI is the practical alternative.
