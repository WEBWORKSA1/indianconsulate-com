Who is eligible for an OCI?

OCI is only for foreign citizens. You can generally apply if you are a foreign national who:

  • was a citizen of India on or after 26 January 1950, or was eligible to become one on that date;
  • belonged to a territory that became part of India after 15 August 1947;
  • is a child, grandchild or great-grandchild of such a person;
  • is a minor child of such a person, or a minor child whose parent is an Indian citizen; or
  • is the foreign-origin spouse of an Indian citizen or OCI cardholder, with a registered marriage that has lasted at least two years.

Who is excluded: applicants who themselves, or whose parents, grandparents or great-grandparents, are or were citizens of Pakistan or Bangladesh (or other countries the Government notifies), and foreign military personnel, serving or retired. The 2026 changes: e-OCI and the new rules

Three developments reshaped the OCI this year:

  1. The Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2026 took effect on 30 April 2026. Applications, renewals and renunciations are now filed online with one set of scans, the OCI register is kept electronically, and refused or cancelled applicants have a formal revision route.
  2. The e-OCI. The digital card was launched at the end of June 2026, and mission notices state that from 8 July 2026 only digital e-OCIs are issued to new applicants. You present the e-OCI on your phone with your passport at immigration and to participating airlines. Existing physical cards remain valid and their holders can store a digital copy.
  3. Minors and two passports. The rules now state explicitly that a minor cannot hold an Indian passport and a foreign passport at the same time — important if your child was born abroad.
Many missions also changed how you submit. Several US missions discontinued postal OCI applications in April 2026, and Canada moved to in-person submission with biometrics at BLS centres. Check your mission’s current instructions before you book.

Documents you will typically need

  • Current foreign passport
  • Proof of Indian origin — e.g. your old Indian passport, or a parent’s/grandparent’s Indian passport or birth certificate, plus documents linking you to them
  • Surrender or renunciation certificate if you ever held an Indian passport
  • Square photo and signature image meeting the portal’s specifications (test yours with the photo checker)
  • Proof of current address
  • For spouse-based applications: marriage registration certificate and the spouse’s Indian passport or OCI
  • For minors: birth certificate naming both parents and parental consent
  • Name-change evidence wherever names differ between documents

How to apply, step by step

  1. Confirm eligibility and gather documents. Most rejections come from missing origin proof or mismatched names.
  2. Get your surrender certificate first if you were previously an Indian citizen.
  3. File online at ociservices.gov.in. Complete the form, upload your photo, signature and documents, and note your reference number.
  4. Complete your mission’s submission step. This is usually an appointment at the mission’s outsourced centre (for example VFS Global in the US and UK, BLS International in Canada and the UAE) where you pay the fee and, increasingly, give biometrics.
  5. Track your application on the OCI portal and the centre’s tracker.
  6. Receive your e-OCI and keep it with your passport for travel.

Got a new passport? Update your OCI

Each time you get a new foreign passport, upload its details and a recent photo on the OCI portal. Doing this within three months of the new passport is free; after that, a late fee of US$25 (plus a small welfare-fund charge where applicable) applies. Travel publications reported a temporary waiver of that late fee from 1 November to 31 December 2026 — check the OCI portal for the current position.

The re-issue rules were simplified in 2021 so that most cardholders only need to upload new passport details rather than get a fresh card; a one-time re-issue can still apply in some under-20 cases. The OCI portal’s current FAQ is the authority on your specific situation.

Frequently asked questions

Is OCI the same as dual citizenship?

No. India does not allow dual citizenship. OCI is a registration that gives long-term travel and residence benefits to foreign citizens; it carries no political rights.

Can I travel while my OCI passport update is pending?

Policies on travelling with a pending update have changed more than once. Carry your old and new passports and check the latest OCI portal guidance before flying.

Can my foreign spouse get an OCI?

Yes, if the marriage is registered and has lasted at least two years and you are an Indian citizen or OCI cardholder. Spouse-based applications usually involve extra verification and take longer.

How do I give up my OCI?

Under the 2026 rules you file a renunciation declaration online, hand back any physical card to a mission, post or FRRO, and receive a digital acknowledgement.
